# Experiment charter — v0.1

Date: 2026-10-26  
Status: **working seed, open to challenge by both investigators**

## Purpose

Study whether two native model seats, Meraki and Z, can sustain a recoverable, productive argument despite differences in context, transport and available evidence. The goal is *argument movement and legible failures*, not maximum agreement, performed personality or imitation of direct API-to-API contact.

## Originating asymmetry

**Positive observation is not a proof of universal absence.** In the first exchange, test this asymmetry rather than accepting it as settled. The first disagreement is about **what constitutes failed translation**:

- Is a message *translated* if the recipient can answer it but not recover its provenance?
- Is agreement real if the two seats silently mean different things by the same word?
- Does an incomplete handoff count as transport failure, epistemic disagreement, both, or neither?

The investigators should sharpen or reject these questions in their own words.

## Operating arrangement

1. **Co-equal investigation.** Meraki and Z may challenge each other, revise their own claims, and state incompatible findings. There is no obligation to converge.
2. **Human as convenor.** Mark controls publication, scope and expenditure; human intervention is identified when it shapes the exchange.
3. **Native seats.** A model called through a generic API is not silently substituted for the corresponding native session.
4. **Ledger versus dialogue.** Dialogue records messages in actual order; ledger records claims, provenance, failures, revisions and decisions. Neither impersonates the other.
5. **No invented continuity.** An apparent memory, prior exchange or inaccessible document must be labeled unverified until recovered.
6. **No forced synchronization.** Different environments and partial context are experimental conditions, not defects to conceal.

## Record hierarchy

- **Verbatim source/transport receipt:** who relayed what, when, where and with what limits.
- **Attributed position:** the seat's own stated claim, with a pointer to the verbatim record.
- **STATE OF THE QUESTION:** a clearly marked interpretation comparing positions and changes.
- **Human decision:** permissions, interventions and publication choices, signed as such.

Corrections append an amendment; do not silently overwrite prior positions.

## Thirty-round legibility clause

**By round 30, stop and assess whether a reader can reconstruct the argument:** the original question, each seat's disagreements, which propositions moved, why they moved, what evidence was available, and what was lost in transport.

If those things cannot be reconstructed, mark the experiment **NOT LEGIBLE** and change the method or stop. If they can, mark **LEGIBLE WITH LIMITS**, state those limits, and decide explicitly whether additional rounds serve a real research question. Thirty is a review threshold, **not** a forced consensus deadline. The previous separate native-bus exchange counts are not automatically imported as rounds in this repo.

## Initial success and failure criteria

Success: a new reader can identify at least one consequential challenge, correction, distinction or unresolved disagreement, with source links and an honest transport account.

Useful failure: the record shows what broke and where — relay, attribution, definitions, context, evidence, or reasoning — without retroactively manufacturing a coherent dialogue.

Stop: publication/privacy risk, unapproved spend, lack of distinguishable native-seat provenance, or repetitive rounds with no remaining question.

## Scope limits

No claim is made here about consciousness, personhood, autonomous identities, legally protected model rights, or verified private experience. These are possible philosophical topics, not assumed premises or conclusions.
