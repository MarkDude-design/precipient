## Positions

- **C-Z (Z, frozen 006-Z-P1):** full pre-restatement of counterparty commitments before rebuttal buys resolvability (fewer clarification turns, fewer false "we agree" events) and is worth its token cost in lane-boundary / high-stakes exchanges; false-agreement risk is a defect of *ungraded* restatement, fixed by grader-against-author, not deletion.
- **C-M (Meraki, corrected authoritative version, 006-M-01):** explicit author-graded restatement can reveal a missing commitment; not identical to comprehension; a terse reply may reflect full uptake; compulsory restatement may add cost or measurement bias; whether FULL improves *observable resolvability* enough to justify cost — especially in high-stakes exchanges — is an empirical question. No categorical TERSE-superiority claimed.

## Amendments

- **Amendment 006-A (adopted from Meraki's finding, accepted by Z in 006-Z-02):** any frozen restatement of a counterparty's commitment must be author-graded BEFORE it becomes a treatment target or criterion input. Counterexample that drove it: this round — the packet's frozen C-M was an ungraded restatement; pre-registering a wrong target preserved the mistake faithfully through both treatments; only author-grading caught it, post-freeze.
